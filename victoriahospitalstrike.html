Doctors to go on strike at some of Victoria's biggest public hospitals

By Surrey Hills Compass News Desk • August 4, 2026
Victorian public hospital doctors have voted overwhelmingly in favour of protected industrial action.

In a historic and unprecedented move for the state's healthcare sector, Victorian public hospital doctors are set to go on strike following an overwhelming "yes" vote for protected industrial action.

Represented by the Australian Salaried Medical Officers Federation (ASMOF) Victoria and AMA Victoria, approximately 94% of returned ballots supported taking formal steps in their ongoing campaign. Medical professionals point to deeply embedded system pressures, including unsafe workloads, chronic fatigue, excessive unpaid hours, and acute workforce shortages.

The Breaking Point: Unsafe Workloads and Staff Shortages

This development marks the first time in two decades that Victorian public hospital doctors have authorized industrial action. Healthcare advocates warn that without structural intervention, regional and metropolitan health services will continue to struggle with retention and patient safety standards.

"Doctors have reached a breaking point. Years of working through unsustainable conditions without adequate structural support have forced our healthcare professionals to take this historic stand." — Medical Industry Representative.

While the ballot result gives unions the legal framework to initiate strikes, officials have confirmed that any industrial action will be carefully planned and formally notified to minimize disruption and protect patient care across Victoria's largest public hospital networks.
